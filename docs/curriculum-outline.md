# Curriculum and evidence map

## 1. An action can have several motives

Distinguish an observed action from a proposed motive and generate a testable alternative.

- Motivation concerns direction and persistence.
- People manage competing demands.
- Use several forms of evidence.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Motivation](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-1-motivation); [Ryan, Deci (2000): Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68)

## 2. Needs and incentives interact

Explain how bodily regulation and environmental incentives can both influence action.

- Regulation can motivate action.
- An incentive is a feature of the situation.
- Useful explanations include context.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Motivation](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-1-motivation); [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Hunger and eating](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-2-hunger-and-eating)

## 3. Value and expectation shape effort

Distinguish valuing an outcome from expecting that effort will achieve it.

- Wanting and expecting differ.
- Self-efficacy is task-specific.
- Changing the route can change the choice.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Motivation](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-1-motivation); [Ryan, Deci (2000): Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68); [Bandura (1977): Self-efficacy: Toward a unifying theory of behavioral change.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191)

## 4. Autonomy is not doing everything alone

Distinguish autonomy, competence and relatedness from absence of structure or universal rewards rules.

- Self-determination theory names three needs.
- Structure can support autonomy.
- Motivation varies in its regulation.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Motivation](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-1-motivation); [Ryan, Deci (2000): Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68)

## 5. Goals need feedback and room to revise

Design a suitable process goal and evaluate a measurable outcome without optimizing a harmful proxy.

- A target directs attention.
- A process plan connects cue and action.
- Revision can be intelligent regulation.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Motivation](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-1-motivation); [Locke, Latham (2002): Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation: A 35-year odyssey.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705)

## 6. Approach and avoidance can coexist

Explain an ambivalent choice without treating avoidance as weakness or approach as universally better.

- The same option can attract and deter.
- Avoidance has a function and a cost.
- Change the choice architecture respectfully.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Motivation](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-1-motivation); [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Anxiety disorders](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/15-4-anxiety-disorders)

## 7. An emotion has several components

Distinguish subjective feeling, physiology, expression and appraisal without treating any one as a unique readout.

- Feeling and expression are different measures.
- Theories explain relationships differently.
- Appraisal links events with concerns.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Emotion](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-4-emotion); [Gross (1998): The Emerging Field of Emotion Regulation: An Integrative Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.3.271)

## 8. Regulation is choosing a response to a context

Compare regulation strategies and select an appropriate option without claiming one strategy is always best.

- Regulation can occur at several points.
- Reappraisal and suppression do different work.
- Evaluate outcomes, not strategy slogans.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Emotion](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-4-emotion); [Gross (1998): The Emerging Field of Emotion Regulation: An Integrative Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.3.271)

## 9. Feelings are also social information

Explain social emotions without inferring a universal meaning from one expression or norm.

- Social emotions concern relationships and standards.
- Expression follows social conditions.
- Empathy does not guarantee accuracy.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Emotion](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/10-4-emotion); [Ryan, Deci (2000): Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68); [Tangney, Stuewig, Mashek (2007): Moral Emotions and Moral Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145)

## 10. A useful change needs more than enthusiasm

Evaluate a motivation or emotion intervention using meaningful outcomes, alternatives and limits.

- Wellbeing has several meanings.
- A testimonial leaves alternatives open.
- Make a modest, revisable recommendation.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: The pursuit of happiness](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/14-5-the-pursuit-of-happiness); [Ryan, Deci (2000): Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68); [Gross (1998): The Emerging Field of Emotion Regulation: An Integrative Review](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.3.271)
